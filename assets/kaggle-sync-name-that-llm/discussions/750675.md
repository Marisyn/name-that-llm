## Content

The recent two-stage LoRA solutions are very inspiring. After studying these approaches, we started looking at a question:
Could Transformer representations contain model-identity information that the current pooling strategies and linear classification heads do not fully exploit?
We are investigating the following pipeline:
Response text → multi-layer Transformer representations → stylometric features and data engineering → nonlinear 18-class classification → ensemble with LoRA branches.
The idea is not simply to replace a linear head with LightGBM. It is to distinguish three signals that may be entangled in the embeddings: response topic, generating-model identity, and collection source or generation settings.
A few questions seem particularly interesting.
1. High-variance directions may not be the most useful directions
Multi-layer embeddings often require dimensionality reduction. But if topic differences dominate the variance while model fingerprints occupy weaker directions, standard PCA could preserve topic information while discarding useful classification signals.
We are considering comparisons between regularized linear heads, classification after PCA, supervised projections, and representations constructed from differences between responses to the same prompt. The interesting question is: can same-prompt responses help identify directions that emphasize model identity rather than content?
2. Within-prompt centering may also remove useful identity information
Centering is often interpreted as removing topic information. However, the group mean also depends on which models are represented and how frequently they appear.
If a topic cluster contains only a few responses, or is dominated by one model family, subtracting its mean may also alter the stylistic signal. Training with same-prompt triplets and performing inference with estimated clusters of varying sizes introduces another potential mismatch.
We would like to compare absolute representations, relative representations, and combinations controlled by cluster reliability.
3. Tree models may be more useful for learning when to trust each source of evidence
Training trees directly on tens of thousands of embedding dimensions may not be the most effective approach. An alternative is to combine:
- class scores from different depths or backbones;
- branch disagreement, predictive entropy, and top-two margins;
- punctuation, function-word, and sentence-length features;
- topic-cluster size and consistency;
into a compact table, allowing CatBoost, LightGBM, or a small MLP to learn conditional relationships.
The question is: can these nonlinear relationships consistently correct errors made by linear heads, rather than merely recalibrating information the heads already capture?
4. Data engineering may determine the ceiling of this approach
Public data provides more responses, but differences in providers, thinking settings, and model snapshots may introduce source-specific signals that classifiers can easily exploit.
We therefore want to examine source weighting, generation-setting matching, and prompt deduplication, using prompt-grouped validation and source-held-out stress tests to distinguish model-identity gains from collection artifacts.
We are organizing these ideas into controlled comparisons: identical folds and post-processing, evaluating linear classification, nonlinear classification, and their net corrections over a strong LoRA baseline. All supervised dimensionality reduction and upstream fine-tuning must remain within the corresponding training folds.
Has anyone observed that the directions best separating topics within a Transformer representation differ from those best separating generating models? Related experiments—including negative results with dimensionality reduction, centering, or nonlinear classifiers—would be very interesting to discuss.
